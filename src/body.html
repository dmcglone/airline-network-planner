Departure-time spacing within a market
Tighter packing needs fewer aircraft and gates; wider spacing gives every market a usable spread of times.

A spoke city earns a morning departure by having an aircraft sleep there overnight. When a station is switched on here, each of its spokes is assigned to whichever enabled station serves it most, and the rotation builder prefers that spoke as its overnight stop — so the aircraft pushes at 05:30 local and lands in time to connect onto the station's bank. MCO is different: it is timed to the 10:00 Caribbean wave rather than a dawn push, and its arrivals are spread over the two hours before it so they don't all need a gate at once.

Turning a station off doesn't remove any flying — those spokes just take whatever times fall out of hub-side packing, which is usually mid-morning or later.

Assumptions Every field is editable and drives the whole rebuild

Block time = ground time (taxi plus climb and descent allowance) ÷ 60 + great-circle distance ÷ cruise speed. Calibrated so SJC–LAX blocks about 1h15 and SJC–EWR about 5h20. Range is a conservative full-payload figure; the A319 sits at 2,700 nm against an Airbus book figure of roughly 3,590 nm at 124 seats. Utilization is the target the rotation builder packs toward — raise it and you need fewer tails.

Airport board Every movement at one airport, with gates assigned from the real aircraft rotations

Gates are not decorative. Every aircraft's arrival and its onward departure hold the same gate for the whole ground period, and no two aircraft ever hold a gate at once — the assignment is a graph colouring over the ground periods in the rotations, so the gate count you see is the real requirement. Status is derived from the current clock at that airport. Simulate irregular ops layers a deterministic delay pattern on top; it is off by default and changes nothing in the underlying schedule.
